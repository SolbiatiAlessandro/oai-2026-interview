# "Why OpenAI" — answer construction

The "why OpenAI" question is asked in nearly every round (recruiter,
hiring-manager, behavioral) and is non-negotiable [S1, S3, S5, S13,
S20]. A generic answer ("I love AI / I believe in AGI") is a
recurring rejection cause [S3, S11, S13].

This page is an answer-construction template, not a script. Adapt it
to your own evidence.

## The four ingredients of a non-generic answer [S20]

A strong answer combines **at least three** of:

1. **Mission specificity.** Don't say "AGI". Say what you think AGI
   does, who it helps, and on what timeline. Reference one specific
   OpenAI artifact (a recent paper, blog post, launch, charter
   passage) and what it tells you about how the org thinks.
2. **Personal proof.** A concrete story that shows you've already
   acted on similar values. Not "I care about safety" — "I delayed
   launch X because of Y, here's what we did".
3. **Network proof.** People you trust who moved to OpenAI and what
   they told you. Anonymized is fine. This is one of the most
   credible signals — it shows you have insider perspective and
   chose to act on it [S20].
4. **Product proof.** A specific use of an OpenAI product where you
   personally felt "this is real". Bonus if it's a non-obvious
   third-party use case (a partner who actually depends on the
   product), not a screenshot of your own ChatGPT chat.

## The four ingredients applied (S20's draft)

- Mission specificity: Sam's *Intelligence Age* essay framing AGI
  as a tool to compress decades of progress on the hardest problems
  (climate, biology, productivity).
- Personal proof: pivoting from a UK SWE role to a climate-focused
  Meta team; building an internal hackathon to repurpose a team
  after a layoff; spending a career arc choosing impact over
  comp/level.
- Network proof: smart ex-Meta colleagues who moved over and told
  S20 directly that the mission focus was real (e.g. Allison working
  on hallucination reduction for ChatGPT users).
- Product proof: girlfriend (therapist) using the Operator-style flow
  to speed up post-session notes — a concrete real-world social-good
  use; "every time I open ChatGPT I feel there's so much to build,
  unlike Instagram" [S20].

## Common follow-ups (and how to handle them) [S1, S3, S20]

- "What about user trust / safety?" → reference an OpenAI safety
  artifact (charter, system card, deployment safety doc) and a
  concrete example from your own work where you slowed down for
  safety / quality.
- "Is AGI the downfall of humanity?" → take it seriously, give your
  own probabilistic-or-not view, name the failure modes you find
  most plausible, name the work you find most credible at reducing
  them. Avoid hand-waving optimism *and* doomer signaling.
- "Mission is ambiguous — how does that resonate with you?"
  → comfortable-with-ambiguity story from your own career, plus
  why ambiguous missions tend to attract people you want to work
  with.
- "Why now, why not 2 years ago / 2 years later?" → honest answer
  about your own readiness, and what specifically about the current
  moment (a model release, a research direction, a team) made you
  reach out.

## Pitfalls

- Comp / level signaling. Mention only if asked, and minimize.
- Generic hype. "It's the most exciting company in tech" is a flag.
- Mission-only with no personal proof; it sounds rehearsed.
- Personal-proof only with no mission specificity; you sound like
  you'd be equally happy at any frontier lab.
- Repeating talking points from a podcast. Interviewers who follow
  the same media will recognize them.

## A 3-sentence skeleton

> *Two sentences of mission specificity (what you think the mission
> is, what specific OpenAI artifact informed your view, what timeline
> you find credible). One sentence of personal-proof + product-proof
> (a story from your career + a real use of an OpenAI product that
> made you feel it's real). Optional fourth sentence of
> network-proof.*
