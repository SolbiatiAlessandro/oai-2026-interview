# Technical project presentation (staff levels)

A round used at L5+ and reported to be unusual relative to peer
companies [S3, S13]. Slide-driven, ~45 min total: ~25-30 min of
presentation, ~15-20 min Q&A. Heavy weight in the staff debrief [S3].

## Format

- **Duration**: 45 min, slides on screen-share [S3].
- **Audience**: usually 1-2 senior ICs / managers.
- **Project scope**: a multi-quarter technical initiative you led. The
  emphasis is **strategic / organizational impact**, not implementation
  details [S3].

## What to include

Recommended structure (compiled from S3, S13 and aggregator guides):

1. **Context** (3 min): what was the problem, who needed it solved,
   why now?
2. **Constraints and decision space** (5 min): what were the technical
   and organizational constraints; what alternatives existed?
3. **Approach and key decisions** (10 min): the design with explicit
   trade-offs at each major fork. Skip implementation minutiae.
4. **Execution and surprises** (5 min): what went off-plan, how you
   adapted, who you collaborated with cross-functionally.
5. **Outcome and metrics** (5 min): impact in concrete numbers; what
   the project enabled organizationally.
6. **Retrospective** (2 min): what you'd do differently; what next.

Leave generous time (10-15 min) for Q&A — the panel will want to
probe one or two decision points at depth.

## Bar / signals [S3, S13]

- **Strategic decision-making**: did you choose the right battles, in
  the right order?
- **Influence and leadership**: did you change the org's mind on
  something material; did you mentor or unblock other engineers?
- **Communication clarity**: can a non-expert in the system follow
  the slides and a peer-expert ask deep follow-ups?
- **Honesty about trade-offs**: openness about what failed and what
  you'd revisit is graded *positively*.

## Pitfalls

- Implementation-detail re-tour. Save it for follow-up Q&A [S3].
- Stories where you weren't the primary technical owner.
- Slides that are wall-of-text vs. one-claim-per-slide.
