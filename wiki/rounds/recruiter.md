# Recruiter / hiring-manager screen

The first live touch-point. ~30 minutes. Conversational. Two flavors —
sometimes one combined call, sometimes split into separate recruiter and
hiring-manager screens [S1, S3, S5].

## Recruiter screen (~30 min)

Goal: confirm fit-on-paper, calibrate level, set expectations.

Typical content [S1, S3, S5, S16]:

- Walk through your background.
- Why OpenAI specifically (not "any frontier lab"). Mission-fit signal
  starts here [S1, S3].
- Compensation expectations and logistics (location, visa, timing).
- Loop preview: what your specific loop will look like, who will be on
  it, expected timing.

What candidates report calibrating recruiters about [S5, S13]:

- Whether the loop includes a system-design screen (varies by IC level
  and team).
- Whether the team uses CoderPad or allows screen-shared IDE.
- Common failure modes / what specifically the team is screening for.

## Hiring-manager screen (~30 min)

Goal: team-fit, technical-direction match, depth-of-experience sanity-
check.

Typical content [S3, S5]:

- Pick one project to walk through in detail. Expect probing at the
  level of "why this design", "what alternatives", "what failed".
- Discussion of OpenAI's roadmap or recent paper, especially if the
  team is research-leaning.
- Your questions for the manager (don't no-op this; comes through as
  low engagement).

## Common signals graded

- Mission alignment with OpenAI's stated AGI / safety mission [S1, S3].
- Communication clarity in 30 minutes [S3].
- Depth of recent technical work, not breadth of resume [S5].
- Sensible questions back, signaling preparation [S3, S13].

## Common pitfalls

- Treating it as small-talk. The hiring-manager call is non-trivially
  calibrated and rejections happen here [S3, S11].
- Generic "I love AI" mission answers. Concrete: which papers, which
  problems, which trade-offs you find sharp [S6, S13].
